## 1. Cover — AO/1

[Screenshot](../assets/examples/user-curated/ao-p001-cover.png)

- **Composition:** full-bleed deep-navy faceted field; small identity/subject line above a very large, left-aligned white title; date low on the left; full Merkle logo near the bottom. The title takes roughly the left half, while the right stays visually quiet.
- **Use when:** opening a substantial brand or business presentation with a short, strong title. Let the title wrap deliberately; make the eyebrow and date subordinate.
- **Adapt:** use an approved background asset or recreate the visual *language* with suitable brand artwork. Check its relationship to the applicable brand-guide version. A simple dark cover or a photographic cover remains possible when the story calls for it.
- **Avoid:** pasting the screenshot as a slide, forcing every cover into three all-caps lines, or copying its old date, copyright, or confidential footer.

## 2. Agenda — AO/4

[Layout reference screenshot](../assets/examples/user-curated/ao-p004-agenda.png) · [Default right-side artwork](../assets/agenda-right-artwork.png)

- **Composition:** use a white field with a small upper-left eyebrow and large, left-aligned section names separated by fine horizontal rules. Align the bundled transparent artwork to the right edge and scale it proportionally to the slide height on the standard widescreen layout. Preserve the original colors and transparency. Keep the list clear of the artwork and the footer visually quiet.
- **Use when:** the agenda contains a small number of concise chapters with a clear sequence.
- **Adapt:** change the number, length, and spacing of rows to the real agenda. A longer or nested agenda needs another treatment. Use confidentiality labels only when the project's actual marking requires them.

## 3. Directional relationship / process map — AO/26

[Screenshot](../assets/examples/user-curated/ao-p026-flow.png)

- **Composition:** aligned columns identify successive domains; short explanatory text sits above the map. Neutral gray nodes show available options; saturated blue nodes pick out one meaningful path. Thin orthogonal connectors and arrowheads make the direction legible.
- **Use when:** the content has a genuine directional relationship across several layers and the audience needs to see one highlighted route through a larger set of options.
- **Adapt:** use as many columns and nodes as the logic needs. Align nodes and keep labels short; use native editable shapes and connectors. Blue should mark the selected or important path only when that status is true.
- **Avoid:** treating every category as a process, drawing arrows that imply unsupported causality, copying the screenshot's example choices, or retaining visible spell-check marks.

## 5. Image-led explanation — AO/46

[Screenshot](../assets/examples/user-curated/ao-p046-visual-evidence.png)

- **Composition:** a clear title and short setup at top; three parallel examples across the body. Each combines one or more overlapping evidence images with a short explanation directly below. A blue lead phrase in each explanation creates a quick scan path.
- **Use when:** two to four capabilities, analyses, or outputs have comparable status and each has a genuine visual example.
- **Adapt:** choose a count and width that let images remain informative; crop or enlarge important evidence, or place detail in an appendix. Keep the explanatory text native and editable; source images may remain images.
- **Avoid:** treating tiny unreadable thumbnails as proof, adding screenshots merely to fill space, or copying example data and claims.

## 6. Timeline with phase explanation — AO/47

[Screenshot](../assets/examples/user-curated/ao-p047-timeline.png)

- **Composition:** shared horizontal time scale at top; blue phase bars sit at their actual start and end positions, with small diamond milestones on a light band. Below, aligned blocks explain each phase. A separate right-side panel groups commercial details and outputs when relevant.
- **Use when:** the audience needs both timing and an explanation of what each phase delivers.
- **Adapt:** show actual units, durations, overlap, dependencies, and uncertainty. Milestones and bars should be native editable objects. Use the lower description area selectively; a high-level roadmap may need less detail.
- **Avoid:** equally sized bars that suggest false duration, decorative milestones, unsupported budget figures, or the source page's spelling/grammar artifacts.

## Shared decisions, without layout repetition

These examples favor strong sans-serif headings, ample white space on analytical pages, a restrained navy/blue/red system, light neutral backgrounds, and content-led emphasis. The consistent design language comes from typography, color roles, alignment, and the Merkle identity. The geometry changes with the information: an agenda is a list, the AO/26 map is a directed relationship, AO/47 encodes time, AO/69 tells a case, and AO/118 connects a buyer to outcomes. Choose the form only after identifying that relationship.
